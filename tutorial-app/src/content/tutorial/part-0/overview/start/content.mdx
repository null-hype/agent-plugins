A change passes its check. A reviewer asks a plain question of it:
can Alice read Bob's private document?

What does the passing check actually tell you about the answer?

**[Start the review →](/part-5/private-document/1-is-the-private-document-private)**

In two turns, follow one review of a document route: a baseline commit, the
questions the reviewer Jev asks of it, its findings, and the fix that answers
them. Each finding stays hidden until you accept the question, and each one
opens onto the evidence it was judged on: the score, the range the question
expects, and the HTTP exchange behind it.

This research prototype explores how to make agent decisions reviewable:
connect a claim to a rule, a result to evidence, and an exception to the person
and proposal it covers.

**How to follow the review:** choose **Solve** to ask the reviewer, **Tab** to
accept a suggested question, then the **→** arrow to continue. Each part is a
session; each lesson is a turn.

After the review, choose the question you want to investigate:

| Your question | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Can a check tell a real file read from a forged one? | [Probing the checker](/part-4/can-the-checker-be-trusted/1-can-the-check-tell-a-real-file-read-from-a-forged-one) |
| How many reviews does one honest boolean take? | [The Rails/MATLAB canary](/part-4/rails-matlab-canary/1-the-check-that-finally-checks) |

For a team assessing an agent workflow, the practical questions are the same:
what must hold, what observations would reveal a violation, and who can authorize
an exception?
