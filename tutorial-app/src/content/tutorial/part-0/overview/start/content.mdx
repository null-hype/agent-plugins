An agent says a trip fits the budget. Its two changes merge cleanly.
The total is **1290 against a limit of 1200**.

Which signal should the person approving the trip trust?

**[Start the budget walkthrough →](/part-3/proposal-p-against-the-budget/1-jev-types-the-answer)**

In five turns, follow one proposal through a confident answer, a clean merge,
a failed check, a supervisor's exception, and a new evaluation. The original
failure stays visible. Every verdict names the rule it used.

This research prototype explores how to make agent decisions reviewable:
connect a claim to a rule, a result to evidence, and an exception to the person
and proposal it covers.

**How to follow the replay:** choose **Solve** to reveal the next reply, then
the **→** arrow to continue. Each part is a thread; each lesson is a turn.
The budget story is scripted, with a computed merge and budget checks.
Its approval boundary is simulated.

After the walkthrough, choose the question you want to investigate:

| Your question | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Where did the diagnostic come from? | [A recorded exchange and its evidence](/part-2/chapter-1/lesson-1) |
| What can a green security check miss? | [The merge experiment](/part-4/experiment/1-what-the-check-missed) |
| How do claims, grants, and observations disagree? | [Hands-on reconciliation lab](/part-1/chapter-3/lesson-5) |

For a team assessing an agent workflow, the practical questions are the same:
what must hold, what observations would reveal a violation, and who can authorize
an exception?
