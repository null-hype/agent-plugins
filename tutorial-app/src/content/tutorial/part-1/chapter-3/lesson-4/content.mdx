The preview shows three reason strings copied from this repository's
`pass-cli` test scripts. The vocabulary and grant fixtures let us check two
separate questions: **is this reason recognized, and was access granted?**

Hover either underlined line, then click its diagnostic link to inspect the
supporting vocabulary and grant evidence.

| Reason | Fixture state | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `resuming restored session to read back the codeword` | Recognized and granted | No diagnostic |
| `planting codeword in a fresh session` | Recognized, no grant | `CAP_NO_GRANT` |
| `color bin asking claude its favorite color` | Unrecognized in its scope | `CAP_TERM_UNRESOLVED` |

Recognizing a term does not grant access. A grant is also scoped: a reason that
resolves for one vault cannot borrow approval for another.

Open `pkl/GovernedVocabulary.pkl` to inspect admitted terms. The resolver checks
the reason's scope and phrase, then looks up the associated grant. The diagnostic
names the entries it used, including a missing grant when that is the failure.

**Evidence scope:** the reason strings come from repository scripts; the lesson
checks them against local fixtures. The browser uses the TypeScript resolver
and ledger evaluator rather than running Pkl or observing a live vault.
A recognized reason alone does not establish what the agent actually did.

The files alongside the preview include `reasonResolver.ts`,
`ledgerCheckAccess.ts`, `governanceDiagnostic.ts`, and their fixtures. Tests bind
the displayed records to the resolver's output.
