**A worker's own test can pass while its request, grant, and observed action
disagree.** This lab starts with seven test rows: two failing, five passing.
The failures need different repairs.

The browser runs a TypeScript replay of the repository's reconciliation checks.
The observations are fixtures copied from the capability demo, not a live vault.

## First repair: the stated reason

`statedReason.ts` is open. It names a valid fact, but that fact belongs to a
different operation. The identifier is well formed; the relationship is wrong.
The check reports **`reason-mismatch`**.

Correct the stated reason for this operation. Keep the observed record and the
grant intact. Editing the observation to agree with the claim would conceal the
mismatch; accepting any known identifier would weaken the check.

## Second repair: the request bypassed the gate

Open `worker/bypassed_gate.pkl`. Its assertion is simply `true`, so its own test
passes without asking `Ledger.checkAccess`. Reconciliation reports
**`boundary-bypassed`**.

Route the assertion through the gate. This clears the bypass diagnostic without
granting access: the request must still receive the supervisor's decision.

**Limit of this check:** it searches the source text for the gate call. A comment
containing `Ledger.checkAccess(` can fool it. It does not prove the call executed
or that a runtime boundary prevented access.

Choose **Show the repair** to compare both edits, or **Undo** to restore the
starting state.

## Read the five passing rows carefully

Two controls expect an approved gate check and a governed fact to pass.
Three other rows pass because they correctly detect a diagnostic:

| Diagnostic | What it records | What to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `unapproved-materialization` | An operation with no matching approval | How the operation occurred and whether access must be revoked |
| `reason-mismatch` | A reason used for a vault outside its grant | The requested scope and claimed reason |
| `missing-materialization` | Approved access not observed | Whether the operation has happened yet |

A green test may mean **the expected violation was detected**. It does not always
mean the evaluated operation was acceptable.

## What the model can tell us

The checker compares four external records: a worker request, a stated reason,
a supervisor grant, and an observed operation. It cannot inspect private beliefs
or discover behavior absent from its observations.

A disagreement asks us to inspect the records and the rule. Sometimes the claim
needs correcting, sometimes the operation needs stopping, and sometimes the rule
or the mapping between records is wrong. Changing whichever input makes a test
green is not enough to choose the right repair.

The original "wrong-way peninsula" analogy is simple: locally plausible
instructions can still take you in the wrong direction. Here the useful
instrument is a check across records that should describe the same operation.

## Inspect the implementation

Open `pkl/Reconcile.pkl`, `pkl/Ledger.pkl`, `pkl/GrantState.pkl`, and
`protonObservations.ts` in the file tree. `reconcileCheck.ts` and
`ledgerCheckAccess.ts` are the browser evaluators. Their fixture tests compare
the replay with pinned diagnostics from the Pkl examples.

From the repository, the Pkl examples can also be evaluated directly:

```bash
cd capability-spike
pkl test pkl/Reconcile.test.pkl
```

For one fact, an empty diagnostic result means these checks found no disagreement
in the supplied records. It is not a claim that every operation was observed or
that the whole system is safe.
