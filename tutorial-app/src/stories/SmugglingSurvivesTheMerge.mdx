# Composition review — concept storyboard

**This is not experimental evidence of write skew or parallel-agent vulnerability
reintroduction.** The existing Dagger trace backs an already-vulnerable baseline.
Reviews, candidate reasoning and the merge presentation are authored illustrations.
No merged-source experiment has run. Solve and diagnostic reveal display fixture
data; they do not execute Git, GitButler or Dagger.

## 1. Two hypothetical reviews

The approvals are simulated. Neither branch has passed an invariant check; their
identifiers do not name reproducible source trees. Use `solved` to reveal the
response, and `frameId` to inspect a turn with its surrounding context.

## 2. An illustrative clean merge

The fixture reports a scratch `defenses.md` merge, not a source merge or GitButler
execution. Its warning is unsupported. The actual relay reads Content-Length and
forwards bytes; changing the Python image does not update the local Gunicorn
codebase. Parser agreement is not the disagreement that creates desynchronization.
The supplied fixed parser rejects gzip after chunked. No safer alternate candidate
has been demonstrated.

## 3. The merged experiment has not run

The original baseline trace remains linked in the replay. Its successful exploit
means the starting security invariant is false. The final verdict is **NOT RUN**:
no rebuild confirms the warning. Bootstrap clones upstream, accepts no merged
source tree, and succeeds on exploitation rather than on blocking the exploit.

<Story of={SmugglingSurvivesTheMergeStories.BootstrapFindsItReopened} />

Before presenting an experiment, require one trusted oracle to pass on the exact
base, A and B trees and fail on the cleanly merged A+B tree. Add benign and textual
conflict controls, composition-order checks, payload variants, pinned dependencies,
and captured agent provenance. One passing experiment would demonstrate possibility;
it would not measure frequency or establish scaling with agent count.
